Interviewer: Thanks for making the time. Can you start by telling me how you first came to use the remote monitoring service?
P07: My GP suggested it after my second admission. Honestly I only said yes because I didn't want to go back into hospital, not because I understood what it did. Nobody sat down and explained the thing properly, I just got a box in the post.
Interviewer: How did you find setting it up?
P07: The instructions assumed I had a smartphone that was newer than mine. It took my daughter about two hours on a Sunday, and she had to ring the helpline twice. If she hadn't been there I would have put it back in the box and forgotten about it.
Interviewer: And once it was running?
P07: Once it was running it was fine, it's just a cuff and the little clip for your finger. What bothered me was not knowing who was looking at the readings. You send your blood pressure off into the ether and there's no acknowledgement. For three weeks I assumed somebody was watching and then I found out it only gets reviewed on a Tuesday.
Interviewer: Did that change how you used it?
P07: It made me take it less seriously, yes. And I started wondering what else they hadn't told me. Where does it go, who can see it, is it on some database. I'm not paranoid but I'd like to be asked.
Interviewer: You mentioned the helpline. How was that?
P07: Forty minutes on hold the first time. The second time we got through quickly but the person didn't know the equipment, she kept reading from a script. My daughter works in IT and she was doing the troubleshooting for her in the end.
Interviewer: Has it changed anything about how you manage your condition day to day?
P07: I think it has, actually. I can see the numbers going up when I've had a bad week with the salt, and that's more convincing than my consultant telling me off. It's the first time it's felt like my own thing to manage rather than something done to me.
Interviewer: Is there a cost to you for any of it?
P07: The kit was free but the batteries aren't, and it wants four of them every few weeks. It sounds trivial but on a pension that's not nothing, and nobody mentioned it when they signed me up.
Interviewer: What about your relationship with the clinical team?
P07: I see the nurse less now, which I'm in two minds about. She was the one who noticed I'd lost weight last year, and a cuff isn't going to notice that. I trust her more than I trust the numbers, if I'm honest.
Interviewer: Anything you'd change?
P07: Tell people what happens to the readings. That's it really. Tell them who reads it, when, and what happens if it's bad. The technology works fine, it's everything around it that's vague.
Interviewer: Thank you, that's really helpful.
P07: No, it's been nice to be asked.
